# MAC Engineering Services — premium landing-page plan

Design direction update: the visual and motion recommendations below are superseded by `MAC-IMMERSIVE-WEBSITE-PLAN.md`, following research into Lando Norris, Terminal Industries and Rotoris. Keep this document as the detailed PDF/content inventory.

Prepared from all 33 physical pages of `MAC.pdf`, with text extraction and visual inspection. Page references below refer to PDF page positions, not the inconsistent printed slide numbers. This is a design and implementation brief; the website has not yet been built.

## 1. Business analysis and positioning

MAC Engineering Services is a Chennai-based engineering services business whose clearest demonstrated strength is HVAC delivery. The portfolio presents broader MEP capabilities alongside design, erection/installation, commissioning, maintenance, onsite support, and operational training. Its offering combines engineering expertise, equipment integration, and project execution.

The PDF lists residential, commercial, and industrial solutions. Its strongest project evidence is institutional and commercial: hospitals, universities, offices, manufacturing facilities, resorts, and industrial sites. Consequently, the landing page should prioritize decision-makers buying substantial building-services projects. Likely audiences include facility heads, project owners, MEP consultants, architects, procurement teams, and main contractors. These audience priorities are a strategic inference from the portfolio.

The main conversion should be **Discuss your project**. The supporting conversion should be **Explore our work**, followed by a downloadable portfolio. A visitor should quickly understand the discipline, see relevant delivery evidence, and reach a practical enquiry path.

Recommended positioning:

> HVAC and integrated MEP engineering, from design to handover.

Creative headline:

> **Engineering comfort. At scale.**

Supporting copy:

> HVAC and MEP solutions for commercial, industrial, and institutional spaces — designed, installed, and commissioned by MAC Engineering Services.

The portfolio's persuasive advantage is the combination of system capacity, execution speed, recognizable project sites, and lifecycle responsibility. Give these facts more space than generic claims such as “premier,” “world-class,” or “unmatched.” Energy efficiency can be discussed as a design priority; the PDF contains no measured energy-saving result to publish as a percentage.

## 3. Creative direction: The engineering within

Make the invisible systems of a building visible. Begin with a beautifully lit architectural volume. As the visitor scrolls, reveal its cooling and service infrastructure, then transition into the buildings MAC has actually worked on.

The visual atmosphere should communicate precision, scale, and calm control. Apple is useful as a reference for clear product storytelling and progressively deeper information. MAC's own buildings, system types, and execution evidence supply the subject matter.

### Visual system

| Element | Proposed specification |
|---|---|
| Main backgrounds | Graphite `#111418` for opening and closing; warm white `#F5F5F2` for readable content |
| Text | Off-white `#F5F5F2` on graphite; ink `#171C22` on light surfaces |
| Brand accent | Deep blue `#2445A8`, subject to matching the supplied logo |
| Diagram accent | Ice blue `#8BCADD` for illustrated airflow, with labels and line style as well as color |
| Red | Preserve in the MAC logo; occasional restrained emphasis |
| Display font | Manrope, weights 500–650, carefully tightened tracking |
| Body/UI font | IBM Plex Sans, weights 400–500; technical figures use tabular numerals where available |
| Desktop typography | Hero 80–112 px; section titles 48–64 px; body 18–20 px; metadata 13–14 px |
| Mobile typography | Hero 40–52 px; section titles 30–36 px; body 16–18 px; fluid sizes checked at 320 px |
| Layout | 12-column desktop grid; about 1280 px content maximum; 64–80 px wide-screen margins; 20–24 px mobile margins |

Manrope and IBM Plex Sans are established variable font families, verified in the Google Fonts repository. This is a fit-based contemporary typography recommendation, not a claim that they lead a measured 2026 trend ranking. Self-host selected WOFF2 files and retain the license files.

## 5. Motion and technical plan

| Tool | Purpose | Constraints |
|---|---|---|
| GSAP + ScrollTrigger | Hero entrance, staged cutaway timeline, section/image reveals, delivery-line progression | One substantial pinned story; normal vertical flow elsewhere; clean up triggers on component unmount. |
| Lenis | Desktop scroll smoothing and synchronization with the story | Integrate with GSAP's ticker using the documented pattern; one RAF owner; preserve keyboard, anchors, nested scrolling, and native touch behavior. |
| Three.js | Building model, schematic service layers, lighting, and camera state | One canvas; load after critical content; bounded rendering resolution; pause offscreen; static fallback. |

Proposed implementation: React + TypeScript with static/pre-rendered landing content. Framework choice can follow the eventual hosting requirements; this page does not need an elaborate application architecture. Load the Three.js scene as an isolated client module. Keep service and project copy in structured data with fields for `site`, `contractingClient`, `location`, `system`, `capacityValue`, `capacityUnit`, `status`, `statusReviewedAt`, `sourcePage`, and `images`.

GSAP controls the timeline state; Three.js reads that state to render the scene. Lenis feeds the same coordinated timing system. Do not enable Lenis auto-RAF while also manually feeding it from GSAP. Bind and remove listeners predictably. Recalculate scroll measurements after assets affect layout.

### Motion specification

- Hero copy: 16–24 px upward movement and opacity over roughly 650–850 ms; a subtle stagger across a few text groups.
- Section entrance: 20–32 px movement over 450–650 ms; once per visit to that content.
- Photography: restrained mask reveal or 1.03-to-1 scale settle; preserve visible subject framing.
- Hover/focus: 150–250 ms contrast/border or arrow transition; feedback works for keyboard users too.
- Cutaway: gradual camera adjustment, controlled layer reveal, and minimal airflow movement. No rapid spin or continuous idle orbit.
- Achievement numbers: readable in their final values on first paint. Optional emphasis animates the surrounding presentation, not a misleading stream of intermediate technical quantities.

### Mobile and accessibility

Use a content-equivalent static story for reduced-motion, WebGL-unavailable, and low-capability paths. Avoid desktop pinning on small screens. Make interactions work with touch and keyboard; project information must not depend on hover. Implement visible focus, skip-to-content, meaningful headings, image descriptions, sufficient contrast, and approximately 44 px touch targets. If project detail dialogs are used, support focus management, Escape, return focus, and independent scrolling.

All text remains readable if JavaScript or an animation fails. Never hide the entire page behind a loading percentage. Forms and navigation remain usable while 3D assets load.

### Performance targets to validate

- Initial non-3D page transfer target: approximately 1–1.5 MB compressed, subject to photography choices.
- Deferred model and texture target: approximately 2–3 MB compressed; reduce complexity if the scene exceeds budget.
- Hero poster: responsive AVIF/WebP, ideally below 250 KB at the common desktop size.
- Use responsive images, fixed aspect ratios, font subsetting, and lazy loading below the fold.
- Cap rendering resolution according to measured performance; explicitly manage drawing-buffer dimensions and update camera aspect on resize.
- Targets: LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 ms, CLS at or below 0.1, and smooth motion on representative hardware. These are acceptance goals, not measured results.
- Stop rendering when the document is hidden or the scene is offscreen; release GPU resources when the scene is removed.

## 6. Asset preparation

Use PDF-derived images for composition prototypes. For production, obtain original, licensed high-resolution site photos with enough room for wide desktop crops and separate mobile crops. Avoid enlarging a screenshot of an entire slide.

Priority assets:

1. Original MAC logo in vector form; match blue/red colors and spacing.
2. Stanley, UIIC, SRM, and Relevantz originals for featured work.
3. Actual plant room, ducting, AHU, installation, or commissioning photos that show MAC's scope more directly.
4. Founder portrait and confirmed public name/title.
5. Approved manufacturer logos and current partnership wording.
6. Custom conceptual building model with named service layers, supplied or commissioned as a new design asset. A simple geometric prototype can establish behavior before detailed modeling.

Any generated or rendered architectural illustration must remain distinguishable from photographs of MAC projects. No invented equipment installation should become portfolio evidence.

## 7. Delivery sequence and acceptance

1. **Content foundation:** preserve every project and source page; reconcile the founder, SRM scope, capacity units, partnership wording, and ongoing status. Unresolved fields can remain flagged in drafts.
2. **Static design:** create desktop and mobile hero, selected-work, and contact compositions; establish type, colors, crops, and spacing with actual content.
3. **Full responsive page:** implement navigation, sections, all-projects view, project details, metadata, and enquiry flow; verify reading order before adding motion.
4. **Motion prototype:** build one short cutaway sequence with simplified geometry; check that it explains services and does not delay access to projects.
5. **Production assets and motion:** replace prototype assets, implement GSAP and Lenis timing, finalize Three.js materials and fallbacks.
6. **Verification:** check 320/375 px mobile, tablet, laptop, and wide desktop; iOS Safari and Android Chrome; keyboard and reduced motion; slow-network poster behavior; no-WebGL path; enquiry success and failure; factual copy against the inventory.
7. **Release preparation:** connect the confirmed enquiry destination, verify contact links, check metadata and canonical URL against the actual domain, optimize media, and review the final public claims. Publishing is a later action outside this planning deliverable.

Suggested analytics events: hero enquiry click, project open, portfolio download, contact link click, form start, successful enquiry. These measure useful intent; any analytics configuration should follow the site's chosen privacy approach. Do not fabricate baseline conversion metrics.

## 8. Research references and limits

Official pages and repository text were retrieved for the references below. The in-app browser was unavailable, so there was no live visual or animation inspection and no Pinterest moodboard review. The creative specifications above are design recommendations derived from the portfolio, not a claimed survey of current trends.

- [Three.js responsive design](https://threejs.org/manual/en/responsive.html): canvas sizing and drawing-buffer management inform the rendering plan.
- [Manrope metadata](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/manrope/METADATA.pb) and [IBM Plex Sans metadata](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/ibmplexsans/METADATA.pb): verified font families, variable axes, and OFL metadata.

The recommended result is a calm, architectural, project-led landing page: an immediate statement of expertise, a purposeful glimpse inside the building, substantial real delivery evidence, and a clear route to enquiry.
